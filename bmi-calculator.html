How BMI is calculated

Body Mass Index is weight divided by height squared. In metric units: BMI = kg ÷ m². In imperial units the equivalent is BMI = 703 × lb ÷ in². The categories above are the ones used by the World Health Organization and the CDC for adults aged 20 and over.

What BMI does and does not tell you

BMI is a population-level screening tool. It correlates with health outcomes at scale but says nothing about an individual's body composition: muscle is denser than fat, so a trained athlete can land in the "overweight" range with a healthy body-fat percentage. Age also matters — after ~70, the healthiest BMI range shifts slightly upward.

  • More informative than BMI alone: waist circumference, blood pressure, blood glucose and lipid panel.
  • Not applicable to: children and teenagers (use percentile charts), pregnant women, or people with edema.

This calculator is for adults and provides an estimate only. It is not medical advice — discuss weight and health questions with a physician.
