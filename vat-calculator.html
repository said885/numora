The two VAT operations, exactly

Adding VAT: net × (1 + rate ÷ 100). A €100 net price at 20% becomes €120 gross — €20 of which is tax. Extracting VAT: gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100). A €120 gross price at 20% contains €100 net and €20 of tax. The two tools above are inverses of each other — round-tripping a number through both returns the original, which is a quick way to check your own invoices.

Rates people search for most

  • 20% — France, UK, Greece (standard rate)
  • 19% — Germany, Ireland (standard rate)
  • 21% — Netherlands, Belgium, Spain, Austria
  • 22% — Italy
  • 23% — Ireland (standard), Portugal, Greece
  • Reduced rates: typically 5–10% for food, books, medicines and children's items — always confirm the rate for your product class before invoicing.

Common mistakes this calculator avoids

  • Adding the percentage instead of multiplying: a 20% markup and 20% VAT are not the same operation — VAT is a multiplier, not an addition to a percentage of a percentage.
  • Reverse-charging the wrong way: to find the net from the gross you must divide, not subtract the rate — subtracting 20% from a gross price gives a number ~3.6% too low at standard rates.
  • Mixed rates on one invoice: split lines by product class and calculate each at its own rate, never an average.

Tax rules vary by country, product class and business status (B2C vs B2B reverse charge). This calculator handles arithmetic only — for filing, follow your national tax authority's rules.
