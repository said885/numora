Two ways to count the same span

The calendar difference (years, months, days) is what identity documents and legal deadlines use: complete years, then complete months, then remaining days. The plain day count is what contracts, shipping estimates and countdowns use. Both appear above, plus the working-day count that project planners and HR departments usually care about most.

Working days here are Monday to Friday, counted from the day after the start date through the end date inclusive. Public holidays are not excluded — national holiday calendars vary too much to hard-code reliably, so subtract those manually if your deadline depends on them.

The traps in date arithmetic

  • Leap days: any span crossing February 29 gains a day. The calculator handles this natively; naive "365 days a year" math does not.
  • Month lengths: "one month after January 31" is February 28/29, not March 3. Calendar-based breakdown avoids the drift.
  • Inclusive vs exclusive counting: a contract "from March 1 to March 3" is 2 elapsed days but 3 inclusive days. This calculator counts elapsed days plus the end date for working days, as documented above.

Dates are interpreted in your device's local time zone. For contractual or legal deadlines, confirm the counting convention the document specifies.
