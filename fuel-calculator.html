One trip, three consumption scales

Europe and most of the world quote consumption in litres per 100 kilometres; some drivers prefer kilometres per litre; the US uses miles per gallon. They measure opposite things — L/100km is consumption (lower is better), MPG is economy (higher is better) — which is why a bigger engine can look "better" when someone switches scales mid-conversation.

Conversions used here: 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 litres, 1 mile = 1.609344 km, and L/100km = 235.214583 ÷ MPG (US). Enter your car's real tank-log figures rather than the brochure number — the difference is usually 10–25%.

What actually moves the cost

  • Speed: highway fuel use typically rises sharply above ~90 km/h (55 mph) due to air resistance, which grows with the square of speed.
  • Cold starts: short urban trips in winter can burn far more fuel per kilometre than the same car's average, because the engine runs rich until warm.
  • Load and drag: roof boxes, towing, under-inflated tyres and open windows all add measurable consumption.
  • The cost-per-kilometre figure is the useful one for comparing against a train ticket or a company mileage reimbursement rate.

Fuel prices and densities vary by country and blend (diesel, E10, LPG); treat the result as a close estimate. Electric vehicles: enter the equivalent consumption in kWh/100 km with your electricity price per kWh.
