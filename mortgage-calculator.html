Reading the real monthly cost

The number lenders quote you — principal and interest — is only part of what housing costs each month. This calculator builds the full picture: the amortized loan payment plus the escrow items most buyers forget at first: property tax and insurance. Enter HOA fees if the property has them.

The formula for principal and interest is the standard amortization: M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where L is the loan amount (price minus down payment), r the monthly rate, and n the number of monthly payments.

Decisions the totals reveal

  • Down payment trade-off: 20% removes PMI on conventional US loans. Below that, add roughly 0.5–1.5% of the loan per year in PMI until you reach 20% equity.
  • 15 vs 30 years: the 15-year payment is roughly 25–30% higher, but total interest is often cut by 40–50%. Compare the "total interest" line, not just the monthly payment.
  • Rate buys more than price: a 0.5% lower rate is often worth more than $10,000 of negotiated price in monthly terms.
  • Debt-to-income: lenders usually want total monthly debts under 36–43% of gross income; many target the housing line under 28%.

Estimates only — taxes, insurance and PMI vary by location and provider, and this calculator excludes closing costs. Your lender's Loan Estimate is the authoritative document.
