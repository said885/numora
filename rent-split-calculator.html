Equal split or income-weighted?

The income-weighted method is simple: each person pays the rent in proportion to their share of the household income. If one roommate earns $3,000 and another $5,000, the $2,000 rent divides 37.5% / 62.5% — $750 and $1,250 — rather than $1,000 each. Enter equal incomes above to see the equal-split baseline for comparison.

Neither method is inherently correct. Equal splits treat everyone as peers and are the default among strangers. Income-weighted splits treat housing as a share of earnings, which most people consider fairer when one roommate is a student, early in a career, or simply in a lower-paid field.

Make the agreement survive the lease

  • Agree before signing. Once everyone has moved in, renegotiating shares is much harder than agreeing upfront.
  • Include or exclude utilities explicitly. Either add them to the total here, or split them separately — but say which.
  • Handle unequal rooms. A common refinement: the larger room pays a fixed premium (for example +$100) that gets deducted before weighting the rest.
  • Put it in writing. A short signed note with the method and amounts prevents the classic "I thought we were splitting evenly" dispute.
