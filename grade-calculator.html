How the weighted average works

Each score is multiplied by its weight, the products are summed, and the result is divided by the total weight entered. With an 80 worth 20%, a 90 worth 30% and a 70 worth 50%: (80×20 + 90×30 + 70×50) ÷ 100 = 78%. The calculator flags when the weights don't yet total 100% — common mid-semester, when some components simply haven't been graded yet.

The final-exam question, answered early

The formula behind tool 2 is required = (target − current × (1 − w)) ÷ w, where w is the final's weight as a decimal. The three outcomes are worth checking before exam season:

  • Required under 100%: achievable — that is the number to aim for.
  • Required over 100%: mathematically impossible; the calculator tells you the maximum grade still reachable instead. Knowing this early lets you redirect effort to other courses.
  • Required at or below zero: the target is already guaranteed — a zero on the final still leaves you at the target.

Some institutions round, curve or cap grades, and some weight by points rather than percentages. When the syllabus disagrees with this calculator, the syllabus wins.
