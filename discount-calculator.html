The two questions shoppers actually have

First: "what does this actually cost?" Multiply the price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). Second, and just as common: "what did it cost before the sale?" — visible on price tags in many countries but not all. Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). Both directions are above.

Discounts don't add up — they multiply

The most expensive mental shortcut in shopping is adding discount percentages. A 20% coupon followed by a 10% member offer is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72 — a total of 28% off, not 30%. Three stacked discounts of 10% each is 0.9³ = 0.729, i.e. 27.1% off, not 30%.

  • Percent off vs amount off: on cheap items, a fixed amount ("$15 off") usually beats a percentage; on expensive ones, the reverse. The crossover is at amount ÷ percent × 100.
  • Two-for-one is exactly 50% off only if you would have bought both anyway.
  • Check the reference price. A discount from an inflated "list price" can be smaller than it looks — the reverse tool above exposes it quickly.
