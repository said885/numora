How the conversion works

Everything routes through one canonical figure — the annual salary — then fans out: annual = hourly × hours/week × paid weeks. Monthly is annual ÷ 12, weekly is annual ÷ paid weeks, and daily assumes a five-day week (weekly ÷ 5). The two assumptions you can and should adjust:

  • Hours per week — 40 is the US full-time norm; 35 is France's legal standard week; part-time contracts vary widely.
  • Paid weeks per year — 52 means every week is paid (typical salaried work). An hourly employee who gets four weeks off unpaid works on 48 paid weeks, which drops the annual figure by 8% against the same rate.

Why the "same" salary can look very different

A €45,000 annual salary in France is €3,750/month before tax — but converting it "per hour" at 35h × 45.6 paid weeks (the French equivalent-time convention) gives ~€21.6/h, whereas the US-style 40h × 52 assumption would show ~€21.6/h only if the hours matched. The hourly figure is only comparable between offers when both use the same hours and weeks assumptions — which is exactly what these two inputs let you align.

Figures are gross (before tax and social contributions). Net pay differs significantly by country and personal situation; this tool converts gross bases only.
