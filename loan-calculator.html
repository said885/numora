How the loan calculator works

This tool uses the standard amortization formula applied to fixed-rate loans — personal loans, auto loans, mortgages and any other loan repaid in equal monthly instalments:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

where P is the amount borrowed, r is the monthly interest rate (your annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments (years × 12). The calculator also shows the total amount repaid over the life of the loan and the total interest — the two numbers that reveal the true cost of borrowing.

Getting a realistic number

  • Use the rate you were actually quoted, not the advertised "from" rate. Lenders set rates based on your credit profile, the loan amount and the term.
  • Check the APR instead of the nominal rate when comparing offers. The APR includes mandatory fees, so two loans with the same nominal rate can cost very differently.
  • Add taxes and insurance for mortgages. This calculator covers principal and interest only; escrow payments are set by your lender.
  • Compare the total-interest figure. Extending a $30,000 loan from 5 to 7 years can cut the monthly payment by ~20% while adding thousands in interest.

Results are estimates for planning purposes and do not constitute financial advice. Your lender's amortization schedule is the authoritative figure.
